90% of young Indian techies use AI daily. Nasscom found only 23% are actually "AI-native."

Why this matters more than your CGPA right now

AI is automating the exact routine, repetitive tasks juniors used to use to build real engineering judgment. That means the entry-level bar just moved from "can you code" to "can you reason, debug, and own an outcome without AI holding your hand." Prompting alone is no longer the differentiator, it is the baseline every fresher already has.

The 5-Question Self-Check

Based on Nasscom's 11-dimension framework, condensed into the 5 questions that separate AI-Proficient from AI-Native. Answer honestly, "yes" or "no."

1Can you tell when AI output is subtly wrong, not just obviously broken?
AI-Proficient people accept fluent-sounding output. AI-Native people catch confident wrong answers before they ship.
2Could you solve the same problem without AI, just slower?
If removing AI access would leave you completely stuck, you have not built the underlying skill, you have only built a prompt habit.
3Do you design the approach before asking AI to execute it?
AI-Native engineers decide the architecture and constraints first. AI-Proficient ones ask AI to figure out the approach too.
4Can you explain WHY your AI-assisted solution works, line by line, to a senior engineer?
Orchestration without comprehension is the single biggest gap Nasscom's report flags in India's early-career workforce.
5Have you caught and fixed an AI hallucination or security issue in your own work, unprompted?
This is the clearest practical signal of independent judgment, the dimension that actually separates the top 23%.

Score yourself

  • 5/5 yes: you are tracking with Nasscom's AI-Native archetype. Keep building real projects, not just more prompts.
  • 3-4/5 yes: solid AI-Proficient, with a clear path up. Focus on questions 1 and 4, explaining and catching errors is the fastest lever.
  • 0-2/5 yes: you are AI-reliant, not AI-proficient yet. That is fixable, start by solving one real problem a week with AI switched off first.
